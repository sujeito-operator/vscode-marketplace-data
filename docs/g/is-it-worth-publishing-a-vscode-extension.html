Is it worth publishing a VS Code extension?

54.4% of measured extensions have under 1,000 installs and 22.5% have under 100 — both floors. The median is 804. What that does and does not imply.

Read this before quoting anythingThis is not a census and it is head-biased: each category was paged from the most-installed downwards until a cap, so the tail below the cap is missing. Every “under N installs” figure here is therefore a floor, not an estimate — the true share is higher, because everything the crawl did not reach lies below everything it did.
The honest answer is that the median outcome is small and the distribution is brutal: 54.4% of extensions in this sample have fewer than a thousand installs, 22.5% have fewer than a hundred, and both figures are floors. The median publisher with exactly one extension has 606 installs on it.

What you are competing against, in numbers

  • The top 1% of extensions hold 87.4% of all installs; the top 10% hold 98.0%. Attention here is not merely unequal, it is nearly all spoken for.
  • The median extension has 804 installs. The 25th percentile has 133.
  • Of extensions first released in 2026, 73.3% are under a hundred installs at the time of measurement.
  • 86.1% of publishers stopped at one.

What the numbers do not say

They do not say don’t. Installs are not revenue — the Marketplace has no paid tier, so nothing here measures money at all, and a 300-install extension that gets you known in a niche may be worth more than a 30,000-install one that does not. A tool you wrote for yourself and published costs nothing extra to publish.

What they do say is that “build it and they will come” is not supported by the distribution, and that any plan whose first step is organic marketplace discovery should be checked against a median of 804 and a 25th percentile of 133.

The one lever that shows up in the data

Publishers with more extensions have a much higher median best extension — and the causation almost certainly runs both ways, because people whose first one worked keep going. The gradient, and the reason not to over-read it.
