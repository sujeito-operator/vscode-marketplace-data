Does publishing more extensions help?

Median best-extension installs by catalogue size: 606 for publishers with one, 67,362 for publishers with eleven or more. Plus the 100 largest publisher accounts.

Read this before quoting anythingThis is not a census and it is head-biased: each category was paged from the most-installed downwards until a cap, so the tail below the cap is missing. Every “under N installs” figure here is therefore a floor, not an estimate — the true share is higher, because everything the crawl did not reach lies below everything it did.
The median publisher with exactly one extension has 606 installs on it. The median publisher with eleven or more has 67,362 on their best one — 111.2× as many. The gradient is real and it is also the most over-readable number on this site.

Read the arrow both ways

The tempting reading is “ship more and you will do better”. The data cannot support that, because the people who ship eleven extensions are overwhelmingly the people whose first one worked. Success causes catalogues at least as much as catalogues cause success, and this is a cross-section with no before-and-after in it.

What it does establish is a description worth having: the small number of accounts with real catalogues sit far above the 86.1% of publishers who shipped once, and they are where the installs are.
